Institute Description

Leibniz Institute of Atmospheric Physics at the University of Rostock (IAP), Kühlungsborn

The Leibniz Institute of Atmospheric Physics (IAP) conducts research on the physics of the mesosphere and lower thermosphere (MLT), focusing on processes and variability in the middle and upper atmosphere. IAP works in close collaboration with national and international partners and connects fundamental MLT research to broader questions related to climate change and atmospheric forcing of space weather.

In the context of MIMAS, IAP provides the scientific environment where model development, interpretation, and comparison to observations (e.g., lidar and in-situ measurements) are tightly linked. The institute’s observation-driven research culture and long-term expertise in noctilucent clouds (NLC/PMC), temperature and wind soundings, and vertical coupling makes it a natural home for MIMAS-family modeling.

Location: Kühlungsborn, Germany
Founded: January 1992
Website: https://www.iap-kborn.de/en/home/

Department Focus

Department Optical Soundings and Sounding Rockets

The Department of Optical Soundings and Sounding Rockets focuses on ground-based optical remote sensing of the middle and upper atmosphere (stratosphere, mesosphere, lower thermosphere) and the scientific interpretation of processes in these regions (and, in part, the troposphere). Lidar systems are a core experimental method, complemented by high-resolution in-situ measurements from rocket- and balloon-borne payloads.

Relevant Research Areas

  • Exploration of the middle atmosphere (MLT) using long-term remote sensing and targeted in-situ campaigns
  • Temperature and wind soundings across mid-latitudes and polar regions to quantify variability and change
  • Noctilucent clouds (NLC/PMC) as tracers of temperatures and fine-scale dynamics in the ~80–90 km region
  • Vertical coupling by tides, planetary waves, gravity waves, and turbulence across atmospheric layers

Key Facilities

  • Lidar systems (including Rayleigh/Raman/Mie and metal lidars) operated at IAP and partner sites
  • Rocket- and balloon-borne in-situ payloads for small-scale structure measurements in the MLT

Scientific Motivation

Why MIMAS?

The mesopause region is difficult to observe continuously and comprehensively, yet it is highly sensitive to dynamics, chemistry, and climate forcing. MIMAS exists to bridge observations and theory by simulating the coupled evolution of an Eulerian water vapour field and Lagrangian ice particles, enabling process-level interpretation of noctilucent clouds and mesospheric ice layers.

Research Questions

  • Question 1: How do transport and turbulence shape the water vapour field and the conditions for NLC formation?
  • Question 2: How do microphysical growth/sublimation, sedimentation, and nucleation control ice particle size and brightness proxies?
  • Question 3: How do waves and vertical coupling influence variability in the MLT, and how can models reproduce observed structures and trends?

Scientific Context

At IAP, MIMAS is part of a broader framework combining long-term lidar measurements, in-situ campaigns, and modelling to understand the MLT region and its response to atmospheric forcing. This includes interpreting NLC/PMC as indicators of temperature and dynamics, and using model studies to investigate long-term change, solar-cycle influences, and coupling between atmospheric layers.
