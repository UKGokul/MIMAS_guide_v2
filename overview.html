What is MIMAS?

One-sentence description: MIMAS is a 3-D mesospheric ice / particle–environment model that simulates water vapour transport and the evolution of ice particles using an Eulerian grid for H₂O and a Lagrangian ensemble for particles.

Model Purpose

MIMAS is a scientific model designed to represent the coupled system of (1) the mesospheric background environment (winds, temperature, density), (2) the Eulerian water vapour field, and (3) a Lagrangian population of particles that can carry dust and grow/sublimate ice. It provides a framework for understanding and testing how transport, mixing (Kz), and microphysics shape mesospheric ice layers and observable cloud properties.

Typical scientific uses include:

  • Sensitivity studies of NLC/PMC ice layers to eddy diffusion (Kz) and transport
  • Testing the role of vertical wind and mixing on layer formation and persistence
  • Linking water vapour variability to particle growth/sublimation in a coupled framework
  • Interpreting observations by comparing simulated layer structure and variability

Purpose & Vision

Problem Statement

The summer mesopause hosts extremely thin and structured ice layers whose behavior is controlled by a tight coupling between dynamics (winds and transport), sub-grid mixing (Kz), and ice microphysics. Fully coupled circulation models can struggle to isolate these mechanisms at the necessary resolution, while box models cannot represent realistic transport.

MIMAS is designed to bridge this gap by prescribing the background atmospheric state while explicitly simulating the evolution of water vapour and a Lagrangian particle population. This enables controlled experiments that separate forcing (environment) from response (H₂O + particles).

Vision

MIMAS is built to support process understanding of mesospheric ice by enabling experiments that:

  • Quantify how transport and mixing set the humidity field that feeds particle growth
  • Trace particle histories (growth, sedimentation, recycling) through a Lagrangian representation
  • Connect model behavior to established literature (e.g., Berger & von Zahn, 2002) and to observations

Unique Features

🔷 Eulerian H₂O + Lagrangian Particles

H₂O is represented as a 3-D Eulerian tracer field transported on the grid, while ice/dust is represented by a large Lagrangian particle ensemble. This hybrid architecture is the central design choice of MIMAS and is the reason transport, mixing, and microphysics can be studied together without turning particles into a purely Eulerian ice mass field.

🔄 Dust Recycling

Particles carry dust nuclei through the system, enabling explicit handling of nucleation availability rather than assuming an unlimited dust background. This matters for interpreting particle origin, persistence, and sensitivity to initial conditions in a Lagrangian framework.

⚖️ Prescribed Environment vs Evolving State

MIMAS distinguishes between prescribed environmental forcing (winds, temperature, density) and evolving model state (H₂O tracer + particle properties). This makes it possible to isolate the impacts of transport/mixing/microphysics without requiring a fully self-consistent circulation model.

📊 Vertical Structure Resolution

The model is constructed to resolve sharp vertical structures relevant for mesospheric ice layers and humidity gradients, using a high-resolution vertical grid and short integration steps appropriate for transport and microphysical response.

Key Assumptions

  • Assumption 1: Background meteorology (winds, temperature, density) is prescribed as forcing on the MIMAS grid; MIMAS does not solve the full atmospheric dynamics itself.
  • Assumption 2: H₂O is represented as an Eulerian tracer on the fixed grid and transported using an advection + diffusion framework (monotonic advection; Kz-based mixing).
  • Assumption 3: Ice/dust is represented as a Lagrangian particle ensemble (super-particles), with particle properties evolved individually rather than as a gridded ice mass field.
  • Assumption 4: Unresolved mixing is parameterized via eddy diffusivity (Kz). Using the same Kz concept across Eulerian diffusion and particle transport is a model design choice discussed in the core framework literature (e.g., Berger & von Zahn, 2002).
  • Assumption 5: Photolysis removes H₂O via solar forcing using a Lyman-α time series input; this introduces an explicit external solar control on the H₂O budget.
Note: Assumptions define the valid scope of model interpretation. Each assumption should be traceable to code and/or key references.

Known Limitations

  • Limitation 1: Limited vertical domain (upper mesosphere only): the model grid spans ~77.8–94.0 km, so lower-atmosphere sources and full-column coupling are not represented internally.
  • Limitation 2: Prescribed dynamics: because u, v, w, T, and density are forcing, feedback from microphysics onto winds/temperature is not dynamically closed unless implemented via external coupling.
  • Limitation 3: Hybrid mismatch complexity: H₂O transport is Eulerian while particle motion is Lagrangian; introducing the same “turbulence forcing” into both requires care to remain physically consistent.
  • Limitation 4: External forcing dependence: results can be sensitive to the quality, cadence, and representativeness of the prescribed background fields and boundary conditions.

Non-Requirements

MIMAS is not intended to:

  • Replace a global circulation model (GCM) or provide fully coupled atmospheric dynamics
  • Provide operational forecasting or real-time prediction
  • Represent the full atmospheric column from troposphere to thermosphere
  • Serve as a complete chemistry–climate model with comprehensive reaction networks
  • Guarantee unique “best-fit” solutions without uncertainty analysis and observational constraints
Focus: MIMAS is a process model: its strength is controlled experiments that isolate how transport, mixing, and microphysics shape mesospheric ice.
